Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Lake Forest

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Lake Forest team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.

What Happens if You Wait

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Cost

How the Quote Is Built

  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.
  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.
