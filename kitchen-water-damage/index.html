Kitchen Water Damage: What Lake Forest Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Lake Forest crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Sink Drain Leaks

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
